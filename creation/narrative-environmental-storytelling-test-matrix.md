# Narrative Delivery and Environmental Storytelling Test Matrix

This instrument tests whether a player can reconstruct a bounded history from spaces, objects, schedules, fragments, testimony, and recurring changes without an omniscient exposition dump or a journal that silently decides what is true.

## Build one inspectable incident

- one recurring landmark with a recognizable function, silhouette, sound anchor, and route role;
- parent, incident, immediate aftermath, and later-mutated room states;
- three involved residents with different locations, access, motives, and knowledge boundaries;
- six fragments across pages, recordings, objects, signage, damage, and resident testimony;
- one honest mistake, one partial account, one apparent contradiction, and one unresolved fact;
- one optional evidence chain that unlocks a related weapon modification;
- one non-missable base weapon and complete no-fragment survival route;
- a journal preserving sources, support, conflict, uncertainty, and placement;
- presentation toggles and accessible substitutes for audio, text, color, and fine visual detail;
- exact state, schedule, fragment-placement, and mutation receipts.

## Instrument the circuit

```text
enter a functioning or previously known place
→ observe authored spatial and material traces
→ discover a fragment within its source boundary
→ record what was witnessed without promoting inference
→ compare another source, time, or room state
→ retain support, conflict, dependency, and uncertainty
→ form a working hypothesis
→ use that hypothesis to change a route, risk, relationship, or counter
→ revisit the landmark after a declared event
→ compare parent and successor environments
→ resolve only conflicts with distinguishing evidence
→ stop when remaining uncertainty has no available witness
```

Mystery is not random contradiction. Environmental storytelling is not clutter. A coherent interpretation remains a hypothesis until independent evidence distinguishes it from alternatives.

## Required measurements

| Area | Minimum record |
|---|---|
| Fragment | identity, source, time, location, audience, witnessed claim |
| Environment | room function, parent state, event, successor state, causal traces |
| Testimony | speaker access, motive, direct knowledge, hearsay, contradiction |
| Ledger | support edges, conflict edges, unknowns, revisions, provenance |
| Landmark | stable anchors, mutation manifest, schedule, recognized differences |
| Discovery | cue, route, missability, lockout, recovery, journal state |
| Reward | evidence chain, related effect, base-path viability, balance impact |
| Accessibility | substituted channel, source identity, timing, meaning retained |

## Test passes

1. **Empty-room baseline:** ask players what the room was for before adding incident traces. The intended function must already be legible.
2. **Causal trace build:** add the incident one event at a time. Every successor element must derive from its parent plus a declared cause.
3. **Clutter control:** build a plausible-looking room with the same asset count but randomized provenance. Authored traces must outperform it on bounded reconstruction.
4. **Fragment isolation:** test every fragment alone. Record exactly what it establishes and reject any wording that exceeds its source's knowledge.
5. **Order permutation:** vary non-causal discovery order. The best-supported account may become clearer, but facts must not depend on arbitrary pickup sequence.
6. **Conflict preservation:** load corroborating and conflicting claims. The journal must retain alternatives until distinguishing evidence arrives.
7. **Repeated-source breaker:** duplicate or paraphrase one account five times. Confidence must not rise as if five independent witnesses existed.
8. **Landmark comparison:** revisit all four room states. Players identify the same place, name meaningful changes, and connect changes to events.
9. **Schedule triangulation:** encounter each resident at multiple times. Behavior, location, and testimony must remain compatible with their available knowledge.
10. **Playable consequence:** remove the narrative chain. At least one route, risk estimate, relationship, hypothesis, counter, or preparation decision should change.
11. **Optional reward boundary:** miss every page. The base weapon and mandatory path remain complete; finding pages grants only a bounded related advantage.
12. **Channel substitution:** remove audio, color, fine detail, and text independently. Critical source, trace, and interaction meaning survives through supported alternatives.
13. **Interruption and reload:** stop halfway through reconstruction. On return, players recover evidence and uncertainty without receiving a generated solution.
14. **Spoiler breaker:** inject an omniscient summary control. It may improve quiz accuracy but must be compared against lost discovery, agency, and replay value.

## Player questions

1. What was this place originally used for?
2. What definitely happened here?
3. Which details are your inference?
4. Who could directly know each claim?
5. Which sources agree, depend on each other, or conflict?
6. What changed since your last visit?
7. What caused that change?
8. What remains unknown?
9. What evidence would distinguish the remaining explanations?
10. What will you do differently because of what you learned?
11. Did the optional reward fit the discovery?
12. Could you finish without it?

Preserve answers separately from authored canon, journal output, pickup order, completion percentage, and designer intention.

## Stop conditions

Do not add more lore pages, recordings, resident dialogue, rooms, timelines, secrets, or codex categories while any remain:

- a fragment states facts outside its source's knowledge;
- the journal erases provenance or promotes inference into observation;
- repeated versions of one source are counted as corroboration;
- unresolved testimony is collapsed into one authoritative account;
- room props imply an impossible event sequence;
- a successor environment cannot be derived from its parent and declared event;
- the room's original function is illegible without exposition;
- randomized clutter communicates as much as authored traces;
- landmark mutation destroys identity or changes only decoration;
- resident schedule, location, and testimony cannot coexist;
- critical evidence depends on one inaccessible sensory channel;
- finding lore changes only completion percentage and reading time;
- the reward has no causal relationship to what was learned;
- a missable fragment contains the only mandatory weapon, counter, or route;
- the journal reveals unseen events, future schedules, or solutions;
- more fragments repeat established meaning without adding a distinction;
- remaining uncertainty has no possible evidence but the game continues pretending it is solvable.

## Selection rule

Retain the smallest set of spaces, traces, fragments, witnesses, mutations, and rewards from which players can reconstruct the same bounded history, preserve justified disagreement, change a playable decision, and clearly state what the world still has not proven.
